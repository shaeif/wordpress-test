Office Wi-Fi Checklist

Walk through these checks with your office manager or IT provider. You do not need technical tools, just a phone, a floor plan and someone who knows who manages your network.

1. Walk and testGo room by room with a phone. Run a free speed test in each space people use.
2. Answer honestlyCircle YesPartlyNo as honestly as you can.
3. Count and actAdd up your "No" answers on page 3 to see where to start first.

01 Coverage

Wi-Fi should work everywhere people actually sit, meet and walk, not just near the router.

  • Every meeting room has a strong signalAt least three bars on a phone, and a video call that does not freeze.
    YesPartlyNo
  • No dead spots in corners, upper floors or back officesMark any room where the speed test fails or crawls on your floor plan.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Calls continue when walking between rooms and floorsStart a Wi-Fi call and walk your usual route. Note where it drops.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Access points are mounted on ceilings, not hidden in cupboardsMetal cabinets, glass partitions and false ceilings block signal badly.
    YesPartlyNo

02 Busy-hour performance

Wi-Fi that is fine at 7am but crawls at 10am usually has too few access points, not too slow an internet line.

  • Speed stays usable when the office is fullRepeat your speed test at the busiest time of day and compare.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Large meetings and training sessions run without complaintsAsk whoever books your biggest room how the Wi-Fi copes.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Your internet plan matches today's headcountPlans bought for 20 people rarely cope with 60 plus video calls.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Laptops and phones connect to the faster 5 GHz bandOn a laptop, the Wi-Fi details screen usually shows the band in use.
    YesPartlyNo

03 Guest access

Visitors need internet. They should never be able to see your printers, files or staff laptops.

  • Guests use a separate Wi-Fi network from staffThere should be two network names, with different passwords.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Guests cannot reach your printers, shared drives or camerasOn guest Wi-Fi, try printing or opening a shared folder. It should fail.
    YesPartlyNo
  • The guest password changes regularlyMonthly is ideal. Former contractors should not still be connected.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Staff personal phones use guest Wi-Fi, not the office networkPersonal devices are not managed by you and should stay separate.
    YesPartlyNo

04 Security and passwords

Your Wi-Fi is a front door. These checks make sure it is locked properly.

  • The staff Wi-Fi uses WPA2 or WPA3 securityYour device's Wi-Fi details will show this. Anything older needs replacing.
    YesPartlyNo
  • The staff Wi-Fi password is not written on the wallOr on a sticky note on reception. It happens more than you would think.
    YesPartlyNo
  • The Wi-Fi equipment's admin password has been changedAsk your IT provider. Factory passwords are published online.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Leavers lose Wi-Fi access on their last dayIf everyone shares one password, change it whenever someone leaves.
    YesPartlyNo

✎ Notes from your walk-through

Rooms with weak signal, slow speeds at busy times, and questions for your IT provider.

05 Equipment and updates

Old equipment slows everyone down and often stops getting security fixes.

  • Your Wi-Fi equipment is less than six years oldThe model number is on a label; a quick search shows its release date.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Software updates for the Wi-Fi equipment are installedAsk when they were last applied. Within the last six months is good.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Someone is told automatically when an access point goes offlineOtherwise you find out when staff start complaining.
    YesPartlyNo
  • The comms cupboard is cool, tidy and lockedOverheating is a common cause of random outages in Doha summers.
    YesPartlyNo

06 Who to call

When the Wi-Fi fails at 9am on a Sunday, everyone should know what happens next.

  • You know exactly who manages your Wi-FiA named person or company, with a phone number someone can find.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Their response times are written downFor example: reply within one hour, on site within four.
    YesPartlyNo
  • Admin passwords are stored safely by your businessNot only by one contractor. If they disappear, you should not be locked out.
    YesPartlyNo
  • There is a backup plan if the internet line failsA second line, or at least a 4G or 5G router for essential work.
    YesPartlyNo

What your answers mean

0 to 3 "No"Your Wi-Fi is in good shape. Repeat this checklist once a year, or after any office move or renovation.
4 to 8 "No"A few weak spots. Start with sections 03 and 04: guest access and security fixes are usually quick and cheap.
9 or more "No"Your Wi-Fi is probably costing you time every day. A professional survey will show exactly what to fix, in what order.
